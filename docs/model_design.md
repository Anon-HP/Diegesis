# Model Design

## Constraints that drove the design

| Constraint | Consequence |
|---|---|
| No public six-class McCloud dataset for Western comics (see `data_sources.md`) | Labels had to be created. The supervised set is small (hundreds of pairs, not tens of thousands). |
| Labels are generated by an LLM (Claude), with κ ≈ 0.63 between two independent LLM annotators | The label noise ceiling is real. A high-capacity model would fit noise, so a simple, well-regularised head is preferred. |
| GTX 1650 with 4 GB VRAM, of which about 2 GB is used by the desktop; about 3 GB of RAM free | Billion-parameter VLMs (Qwen2-VL, LLaVA, etc.) cannot run locally at a usable speed. End-to-end fine-tuning of a large backbone is out of the question. |
| Extreme class imbalance (moment, aspect and non-sequitur are rare in Golden Age comics) | Class-weighted losses, targeted mining of rare classes, macro-F1 and balanced accuracy as the primary metrics. |
| The inference interface takes only the two images | All text comes from internal OCR (Tesseract). Nothing external is used at inference. |

## Architecture: frozen encoder + pair features + light classifier head

```
Panel A ──┐                                   ┌─> OpenCLIP ViT-B/16 image tower (frozen, fp16) ─> eA
          ├─ letterbox to square ─────────────┤
Panel B ──┘                                   └─> ...                                          ─> eB
Panel A/B ─> Tesseract OCR (eng, psm 11) ─> text ─> OpenCLIP text tower ─> tA, tB ; cue-word flags
Panel A/B ─> low-level stats (32×32 thumbnail correlation, HSV histogram L1, edge density, aspect ratio)

Panel A/B ─> DINOv2 ViT-B/14 (frozen, fp16, 224px) ─> CLS + 16×16 patch tokens

Feature groups (src/transition/features.py): any "+"-joined combination can be selected by CV
  clip      [eA, eB, eA⊙eB, |eA−eB|, cos]        clipdiff  [eA⊙eB, |eA−eB|, cos]
  cos_c     mean-centred CLIP and DINO cosines (corpus means from dev panels, stored in the head)
  match     DINOv2 patch-matching stats (best-match quantiles, mutual-NN fraction, displacement)
  zs        CLIP zero-shot scores for 20 shot/content prompts on A and B, plus B−A (prompt embeddings stored in head)
  dino      DINO CLS product/difference;   text  CLIP text-embedding difference of OCR text
  low       thumbnail correlation, HSV-histogram L1, edge density, aspect ratio
  cue/cue2  OCR caption-cue keywords / lexicon families (time jump, place jump, simultaneity, story start)

DEPLOYED (models/head.joblib): clipdiff+zs+cos_c+match+low+cue2
            ─> StandardScaler ─> PCA(64) ─> class-balanced LogisticRegression(C=0.5) ─> softmax over the 6 labels
```

- **Encoder:** OpenCLIP ViT-B/16 (`laion2b_s34b_b88k`). It is about 150M parameters and about 300 MB in fp16,
  so it runs on the 4 GB GPU with room to spare, and on CPU as well. CLIP-style embeddings capture
  "same character / same setting / same composition", which is the main visual signal separating
  moment, action, subject and scene transitions.
- **Pair interaction:** the elementwise product and the absolute difference are the standard Siamese pair
  features. Combined with a cosine scalar, they let a linear model express "how similar, and in which ways".
- **Text:** captions such as "LATER…" or "MEANWHILE…" are strong scene-to-scene cues. OCR on
  hand-lettered Golden Age comics is noisy, so the text features are kept coarse: cue flags, word overlap,
  and CLIP text embedding differences.
- **Head:** we compare logistic regression (with and without PCA), a one-hidden-layer MLP, and gradient-boosted
  trees across feature subsets (image only, image + small features, full). The choice is made by
  StratifiedGroupKFold over books, on the dev split only (train + val).
- **Output:** the argmax over exactly six classes. The confidence is the softmax probability. Classes
  missing from the training data still get probability 0 and are never invented.

## Alternatives considered and rejected

- **Large VLM, zero-shot or fine-tuned (LLaVA, Qwen2-VL 2B/7B, Idefics):** these do not fit the
  GPU and RAM budget at a usable speed. Quantised 2B models leave very little headroom, need
  multi-image prompting, and their zero-shot McCloud judgements are untested. A cloud API was
  ruled out because it costs money and would mean uploading comic images.
- **Fine-tuning the CLIP backbone:** with a few hundred noisy labels it would overfit, and 4 GB of
  VRAM makes it impractical. The frozen backbone plus a head is the defensible choice at this data
  scale.
- **Cross-attention fusion transformer:** it adds capacity the data cannot support.

## Baselines

- Majority class.
- A hand-written rule system (`src/transition/rules.py`) whose thresholds were fixed before any labels were seen.
  It is also the inference fallback when no trained head exists.

## Training data composition

See `data/manifest.csv` and `STATUS.md` for the actual counts. The training data consists of:

- `llm` labels on natural consecutive pairs (Claude, following `annotation_guidelines.md`).
- `synthetic` non-sequiturs: panel A and panel B taken from two different books in the same split.
  These are weak supervision only and are excluded from the headline metrics.

## Final selection and what the experiments showed

The full log is in `experiments/README.md`.

- **Selection rule.** The deployed configuration is the one with the best cross-validated macro-F1 over the
  well-supported classes (≥10 examples), with balanced accuracy as the tie-breaker. The rule was fixed
  before the test split was evaluated, and the test split was evaluated once.
- **Plateau.** Across 18 configurations on 788 labels, every one falls within 0.33–0.38 macro-F1 for the
  supported classes. The deployed configuration's lead over a plain `clip+low+cue` logistic regression is
  within noise. The ceiling comes from the data and labels, not from the choice of head:
  - LLM–LLM agreement is κ = 0.64, and the model is right on 51% of pairs the annotator was confident
    about against 9% of the low-confidence ones.
  - The rare classes have almost no examples.
- **What helped.**
  - Mean-centring embeddings before computing cosines. Raw CLIP/DINO similarities saturate on Golden Age
    art; centring raised the AUC for unrelated pairs from 0.84 to 0.95.
  - CLIP zero-shot shot-type scores. "Establishing shot" scored on panel B gives an AUC of 0.67 for
    scene changes.
- **What did not help.**
  - DINOv2 global features and patch matching, beyond CLIP.
  - Gradient boosting or an MLP instead of logistic regression.
  - Training only on confident labels.
  - Tesseract settings tuning: word recall 0.70–0.73 across settings.
  - Active mining for rare classes: no better than random sampling.
- **The main remaining bottleneck.** Annotators cite caption text in 83% of scene changes, but keyword
  matching on OCR recovers a cue in only 23% of them (34% even with Google Vision OCR). Reading and
  *understanding* captions requires a capable vision-language model. One with 2B+ parameters does not fit
  this machine (4 GB GPU, ~2 GB free), so this is the clearest path to a stronger model on better hardware.
- **Inference cost.** CLIP + DINOv2 + Tesseract. About 2 s per pair once loaded; about 25–30 s for a cold
  CLI start.
